The Universal Lossless Tokenizer represents a new approach to tokenization grounded in representational invariance theory. Unlike traditional tokenizers that make hard segmentation decisions based on frequency statistics, this system treats tokenization as a dynamical control problem.

At its core, the tokenizer implements equilibrium projection: each byte span is mapped to a latent representation, which is then iteratively refined via a contraction mapping until it converges to a stable attractor. These attractors correspond to tokens in the codebook.

The key insight is that tokenization boundaries should be placed where the representational geometry is locally flat - regions of low curvature indicate stable, consistent representations that will generalize well. Conversely, high-curvature regions signal inconsistency and potential failure modes.

The Harmonizer control loop monitors rolling statistics and adjusts parameters to maintain operational stability. When curvature tails grow too heavy or token churn increases, the controller intervenes to restore equilibrium.

This approach offers several advantages:
1. Lossless reconstruction via residual coding
2. Principled handling of arbitrary byte sequences
3. Adaptive behavior across domains
4. Theoretical grounding in dynamical systems

The implementation uses PyTorch for the neural components and pure Python for the control logic. All operations are deterministic given a fixed seed, enabling reproducible research.

Natural language processing has long struggled with the tension between fixed vocabularies and open-ended text. Subword tokenization methods like BPE and WordPiece offer a compromise, but they remain fundamentally statistical - they capture what is frequent, not what is stable.

The curvature-based approach inverts this logic: segment where representations are geometrically consistent, regardless of frequency. A rare but structurally coherent span deserves its own token just as much as a common one.

Consider the word "internationalization" - a traditional tokenizer might split it into subwords based on training corpus statistics. But if the latent representation of the full word is stable and low-curvature, why split it? The Universal Tokenizer makes this decision based on geometry, not counting.

This has implications for multilingual and cross-domain applications. A tokenizer trained on English will struggle with Chinese or code because the frequency distributions differ. But geometric stability is more universal - a well-formed Chinese character or a syntactically valid code block will exhibit low curvature in any sufficiently expressive representation space.

The streaming architecture enables online tokenization of unbounded inputs. The segmenter maintains a sliding window and commits boundaries when confident. Lookahead provides robustness against local optima.

Error handling follows the principle of graceful degradation. If the encoder produces a high-curvature representation, the segmenter can fall back to shorter spans. If the codebook lacks a good match, the residual coder captures the difference. The system never fails silently - it quantifies its uncertainty through the curvature diagnostic.

Future work will explore learned curvature estimators, hierarchical tokenization, and integration with language model training objectives. The framework is designed to be extensible while maintaining its core guarantees.
